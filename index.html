How an LIC 624 unusual incident report is prepared

A six-bed residential care facility for the elderly is usually run by the owner or a small team. When an unusual incident happens, the administrator writes an LIC 624 unusual incident report. The facts are already in the caregiver's shift note; the work is turning them into the report and keeping everything in order for the next licensing visit.

The LIC 624 is the unusual incident/injury report form of California Community Care Licensing. Under Title 22, § 87211(a)(1), an RCFE sends a written report to the licensing agency and to the resident's responsible person within seven days of events such as a death, an injury that needs medical treatment or an unusual incident that threatens a resident's welfare; outbreaks, poisonings, catastrophes and major accidents are reported within 24 hours. What belongs in the report and how it differs from an ordinary note is answered in the questions below; you can add your facility at the end of the page.

CareLog Six starts with the shift note. Caregivers write short notes per resident on a phone or tablet: what happened, when, who was present, what was done. When a note describes an incident, it is flagged for the administrator.

From the flagged note the service drafts the LIC 624: the resident, the date and time, the description of what happened, the immediate action taken and who was notified. The administrator reviews the draft, adds the follow-up and any missing details, and saves it as a PDF to send. The original note and the final report stay linked.

Every resident has a file: shift notes, incident reports, care documents and anything you upload. When you need to show the history of one resident, you export the file in one click instead of searching through binders.

Before a licensing visit, a checklist shows what an evaluator usually asks to see and what is missing or out of date in your files. The checklist is a working aid for your preparation, not an official list from the licensing agency.

The service is built for small homes with up to six residents, where one administrator does the paperwork alongside everything else. Caregivers need only a phone; there is nothing to install.

Access is by role: caregivers write notes, the administrator reviews and sends reports, and the owner sees everything. Records are kept in the facility's account.

Notes can be written in plain language; caregivers do not need to know the form. The administrator sees all flagged notes in one list, with the ones still waiting for a report at the top, so nothing stays in a notebook over the weekend. Corrections are kept with the date and the name of the person who made them, which keeps the history of each report clear. Every report and every note can be found again by resident, by date or by type of incident.

Questions

What is an unusual incident report?

A written report a licensed care facility makes when something out of the ordinary happens to a resident — for example an injury that needs medical treatment or an incident that threatens the resident's welfare. For California RCFEs it is made on form LIC 624, the unusual incident/injury report.

When is the LIC 624 unusual incident report due?

The written report goes to the licensing agency and the resident's responsible person within seven days of the event (Title 22, § 87211(a)(1)); outbreaks, poisonings, catastrophes and major accidents are reported within 24 hours.

What goes into an LIC 624 unusual incident/injury report?

The resident, the date and time, what happened, who was present, the immediate action taken, who was notified, the physician's findings and treatment if any, and how the matter was followed up.

Can you show an example of an unusual incident?

A resident slips in the bathroom at night with no visible injury, and the family and physician are called in the morning. The example on this page shows how such a shift note becomes an LIC 624 draft.

Does it send the LIC 624 for me?

No. It drafts the report from the shift note; the administrator reviews it and sends it.
